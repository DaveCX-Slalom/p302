# BRIEF.md: P302 Interactive Data Story
## "Why Do Patients Come Back?" (working title)

## 1. Industry, audience, point of view
- **Industry:** Healthcare (hospital operations)
- **Primary user:** Dana, a hospital COO / quality director preparing for a quarterly review. Reads on a laptop, occasionally on a tablet in meetings. Not a data analyst.
- **Point of view (the story):** The overall 30-day readmission rate looks flat and acceptable. It hides the real story: two conditions (heart failure and COPD) account for most readmissions, and the spikes cluster on weekends and under-staffed discharge shifts. A small staffing change would remove a large share of them.
- **What Dana should walk away knowing:** "Our problem isn't everywhere. It's two conditions and one shift pattern, and I can act on it."

## 2. Data (invented, no client or Slalom data)
- ~24 months of monthly data, 6 conditions, 3 discharge shifts (weekday day, weekday evening, weekend).
- Fields: `month, condition, shift, discharges, readmissions, rate (derived), nurse_ratio` (patients per discharge nurse; higher = thinner staffing).
- Generated by a seeded script (`/data/generate.js`) so the story is reproducible. State clearly in the UI footer: "Illustrative data."
- Planted pattern: the weekend shift's nurse ratio is about 40% worse, and readmissions for HF and COPD are about 2x there.

## 3. Narrative structure (scroll-driven, 6 beats)
1. **Hook:** "Readmissions held steady at 14.2%. Good news?" (single flat line)
2. **Break it apart:** the line splits into 6 conditions. Two lines are rising, and the others mask them.
3. **Where:** the two conditions broken out by discharge shift. The weekend bar stands out.
4. **Why (correlation):** scatter of nurse-to-patient ratio against readmission rate. Annotation explains correlation, not proof.
5. **What if:** slider adds 0 to 3 discharge nurses on the weekend shift. Projected readmissions and avoided-cost update live.
6. **Takeaway and next step:** three concrete recommendations and a "copy summary" button.

## 4. Interactions (secondary to story)
- Scroll-triggered chart transitions (respect `prefers-reduced-motion`).
- Condition filter chips (affects beats 2 to 4).
- "What if" staffing slider (beat 5).
- Hover/tap tooltips with plain-language explanations.

## 5. Design direction
- **Tone:** calm, clinical, trustworthy. Not flashy.
- **Palette:** off-white background, deep teal primary, a single warm accent (coral) reserved for "the problem". Everything else is grey.
- **Type:** a humanist serif for headlines (story voice), a clean sans for chart labels and UI.
- **Hierarchy:** one idea per screen, a large headline with a single sentence of takeaway, and charts with direct labels instead of legends.
- **Accessibility:** colour-blind-safe pairs, AA contrast, keyboard-operable slider, chart text alternatives.

## 6. Edge cases and Go-further
- Empty state when a filter combination has no data.
- Loading and error state if data fails to load.
- Responsive: charts reflow at 375px, 768px, and 1280px, and stacked annotations replace side annotations on mobile.
- Reduced-motion fallback: static charts with all beats visible.

## 7. Stack and deploy
- Vue 3 + Vite, D3 (scales/paths) or Chart.js, Pinia only if needed, VueUse `useIntersectionObserver` for scroll beats.
- Deploy to Vercel. The reviewer note asked for password protection, but the project is fictional and holds no real data, so the site is public (see section 11).

## 11. As built (differences from the plan above)
- **Charts:** D3 (scales and paths) rendering SVG in three components: `TrendChart` (beats 1 and 2), `BarChart` (beats 3 and 5), `ScatterChart` (beat 4).
- **Data:** `rate` is a derived field. The app fetches `public/readmissions.json` at runtime so it can show loading and error states. `npm run data` regenerates it (seed 2024).
- **Chips:** the condition chips appear in beats 2, 3 and 4 and share state. Beats 3 and 4 only show heart failure and COPD, so hiding both gives an empty state.
- **Beat 4:** the scatter shows heart failure and COPD rows only (r = 0.86). Across all conditions the link is weak (r = 0.44), so including them would blur the story.
- **Beat 5:** a linear estimate from one pooled HF + COPD slope, on weekend discharges from the last 12 months. It assumes 8 weekend discharge nurses today and $15,000 per readmission, both illustrative and shown on screen. The projection never drops below the weekday rate.
- **Beat 6:** the three recommendations use the slider value from beat 5 (minimum 1). Copy summary uses the clipboard and falls back to a selectable text box.
- **Tooltips:** the hover/tap tooltips became a readout line under each chart, which also works with arrow keys.
- **Deploy:** Vercel, public. Password protection was dropped on purpose: the hospital and every number are invented, so there is nothing to protect. A short-lived Netlify password gate was built and then removed.
- **Annotations:** notes drawn on the charts (beat 1: the flat range; beat 2: the other four conditions masking the rise; beat 3: a dashed weekday-level line across the weekend bar with the multiple). They are hidden on narrow screens, where the text beside the chart carries the same point. An abstract heartbeat line fills the intro on wide screens.
- **Context added after the first deploy:** sticky header with a fictional hospital brand ("Northfield General", tagged Fictional), jump links with scroll progress, an intro with who the briefing is for, data facts computed from the data and a glossary, numbered "n of 6" labels, a two-column layout on wide screens, alternating section bands, final values on chart labels, and a fuller footer.
- **Checks run:** no horizontal overflow at 375, 768 and 1280px; an automated axe audit (WCAG 2 A/AA and best practices) found no violations at those widths or with reduced motion on.
